# Day 1 — Define the product and initialize the repository

## Completed

- Defined the initial user: an owner or stock manager of a small grocery shop.
- Defined the daily decision: tomorrow's perishable purchases under stock, expiry, and budget constraints.
- Documented the distinction between observed sales and demand hidden by stockouts.
- Scoped the first workflow to 20–50 products, next-day forecasting, and human-approved draft orders.
- Recorded data limitations, provisional stack choices, acceptance criteria, and questions for a shop owner.
- Added the complete 120-session roadmap and a commit/contribution workflow.
- Initialized Git on `main` with exclusions for local secrets, raw data, and generated artifacts.

## Evidence and limits

The repository currently contains documentation and conventions. No application, model, evaluation result, shop interview, or pilot has been completed. A proposed user need is not yet validated demand for the product.

Day 1 commits group three deliverables: repository conventions; problem and scope; roadmap and contribution workflow. Review actual hashes with `git log --oneline`.

## Decisions

- Start with one grocery use case rather than multiple business types.
- Establish simple forecasting and ordering baselines before more complex methods.
- Keep decisions reviewable by a human; do not automate purchases.
- Separate benchmark results, synthetic operational assumptions, and future pilot evidence.
- Delay application scaffolding until the early discovery milestones in the roadmap are complete.

## Day 2: learn how ordering works in practice

Use the five interview questions in [the problem statement](../problem-statement.md). Speak with an owner if possible; ask for their experience rather than pitching assumed benefits. Do not record their name or private business data in a public repository without permission.

Deliverable: anonymized interview notes covering current ordering, available records, binding constraints, and whether the proposed workflow fits. If access is unavailable, prepare the interview guide and record the access limitation. Never invent an interview.

Suggested next commit, after completing the work: `docs: capture grocery ordering interview findings` or `docs: prepare owner interview and record validation gaps`.
